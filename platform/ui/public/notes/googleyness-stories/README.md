## Story structure (STAR)

- **S**ituation — context (15 sec)
- **T**ask — your responsibility (10 sec)
- **A**ction — what YOU did, 3-5 bullets (60 sec)
- **R**esult — outcome with metrics + learning (30 sec)

**Target: 90-120 seconds per story**

## What makes a strong story

1. **You're the protagonist** — "I" not "we"
2. **Quantified results** — %, $, time, scale
3. **Decision-making shown** — why did you choose X over Y?
4. **Growth signal** — what did you learn?
5. **Recent** — within last 2-3 years
6. **Specific** — names, dates, numbers, not vague

## What to AVOID

- ❌ Stories you weren't the primary owner of
- ❌ Fabricated metrics
- ❌ Stories >3 years old (interviewer asks "why not something recent?")
- ❌ Blaming others
- ❌ "We just did it" (where's YOUR contribution?)
- ❌ All success stories — need at least one good failure story

## Practice routine

For each story:

1. **Tell it once out loud** — time yourself
2. **Record yourself** (phone voice memo) — listen back
3. **Identify weak spots**:
   - Too long? (>2 min)
   - Missing metrics?
   - Unclear what YOU specifically did?
4. **Re-tell, shorter** — 3-5 times per story
5. **Have a friend/peer ask follow-ups** — practice the tough ones

## Variations

For each story, prep 2-3 different "openings":

- **Standard:** "Tell me about a time..."
- **Pivot:** "That reminds me of..."
- **Specific:** "Tell me about a time you faced [exact attribute]..."

This lets you use the same story for multiple questions.

## Common follow-ups Google asks

- "Why did you choose X over Y?"
- "What would you have done differently?"
- "How did people react?"
- "What did your manager say?"
- "What happened next?"
- "What did you learn?"
- "Did anyone disagree?"

**Always have answers ready for these.**

## Bonus: 1-2 backup stories per attribute

If your main story doesn't fit a specific question, have a backup. After 6 stories × 2 backups = 12 stories ready.
